Vardan Voskanyan

Research on self-improving AI systems, London

I work on AI systems that improve themselves, and on making that improvement reliable. Most of my attention goes to the system around the model: how candidate solutions are generated and selected, how an agent stores and reuses its experience, and how its outputs are checked so that it cannot fool itself.

In practice this means search methods for agents (evolutionary and population-based search, tree search, Bayesian optimization), memory and knowledge structures, and critic models with safeguards against reward hacking. My main testbed is code and inference optimization, where results can be measured, with open problems in mathematics and science as the harder target.

I like problems that can be stated precisely and checked, and I like explaining them with pictures. Mathematics is still how I think: optimal transport, control and geometry keep turning up in my work and in side projects, such as a Rubik's cube with a continuum of layers.

I lead research at TurinTech in London. Before that I was a mathematician, with a PhD on mean-field games and optimal control.
